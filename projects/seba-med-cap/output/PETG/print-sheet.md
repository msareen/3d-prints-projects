## PETG slicer settings

Tougher and more flexible than PLA. Snaps tolerate about 2 to 4 % strain. Takes about 70–80 °C. Strings, and oozes slightly fatter than PLA.

| Setting | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Nozzle temperature | 235 °C first layer, 230 °C after (220–250) | good layer bonding for snaps and latches |
| Bed temperature | 75 °C (70–80) |  |
| Bed surface | textured PEI, or smooth PEI with glue stick / release agent | PETG can bond to smooth PEI and tear it |
| First layer | 20 mm/s, 100 % width, Z offset +0.02 to +0.05 | do not squish PETG: elephant foot closes bed-side holes and grooves |

## Part notes

**Accepted printcheck warnings**
- **layer_alignment:** the slot floor (5.7), sleeve rim (10.7) and lid clip top (5.05) sit 0.05–0.1 off a layer. The slicer rounds them.
- **small_holes:** the 2 mm gap between the hinge pin and the skirt is the clip's swing path, not a fastener hole.
- **thin_walls (base):** a few samples on the edges of the sleeve flex slots, where the 45° lip chamfer meets the slot wall. This is a sampling artifact, not a real wall.

**Printing**
- The groove around the 5 mm spout has a short 1.6 mm bridge as its roof. Let the slicer bridge it. No supports.
- Seam: rear, away from the latch (+X) and the hinge (−X).

**After printing**
1. Check that the hinge pin is free between the knuckles. Trim any strings.
2. Lay the lid open flat beside the base, with the clip mouth over the pin, and press the clip onto the pin.
3. Fold the lid over and press the tab. The plug, the seal ring and the latch click shut.
4. Push the base onto the bottle neck until it clicks over the bead.

**Test fit first**
- The snap height (D4, 7.0 mm from the neck top to the underside of the bead) is a photo estimate. If the base won't click on, or sits loose, measure D4 and reprint the base.
- If the latch is too stiff or too weak, change `LATCH_DEPTH` (D38) or `LATCH_PRELOAD` (D45). The values per material are in the print-notes for each material.
- If the lid is too tight to close, reduce the plug bulb `PLUG_BULB_D` (D51) by 0.1 first, then set `RING_FIT` (seal ring) to `FITS["sliding"]`.
- The plug bore (2.9) shows as a "small hole" warning. It is the hollow core that lets the plug flex, not a fastener hole.
- Rev 5: the skirt now reaches the shoulder (no gap). If it stops the cap clicking fully onto the bead, sand the rim, or lower `SKIRT_EXT` (D43, 8.0) by 0.2.
- Rev 5: the seal tube is 4 mm long and goes into the neck bore to stop the wobble. Push the cap on straight.

The seal stops splashes but won't hold pressure. Don't squeeze the bottle hard with the lid closed. Let the parts cool before you snap them together.

## PETG part notes

- **PETG is the primary design.** Fits are 0.05 mm looser per side than PLA, because PETG prints slightly fatter. The seal ring bore is 0.15 per side on the 7.4 spout.
- **Plug (rev 8):** hollow, 3.0 long, 1.0 wall, with a 5.0 bulb (the same as the channel) and a taper to a 4.5 tip. The click comes from print growth: PETG prints the hole about 0.1 small and the plug about 0.1 large. Seated, the bulb springs fully out into the 5.2 groove, so it is not squeezed when the lid is shut. If it is too loose, step the bulb up by 0.05 (5.05, 5.1). Don't jump by 0.1.
- **Latch:** bead 0.5 with a 0.2 hook preload. Opening and closing bends the arm to 3.0 % strain, inside PETG's 2-4 % limit.
- If the lid is now too stiff, lower `LATCH_DEPTH` (D38) to 0.4 first. Alternatively put measured clearances in `processed/fits-PETG.json`.
- Clear strings from the hinge pin, the 5 mm hole and the spout groove before assembly.
- PETG takes about 70-80 °C, so the cap survives a hot bathroom or a hot car.

## PETG handling

- Dry the filament before printing (65 °C for 4–6 h). Wet PETG strings, pops and prints rough fits.
- Clean strings off pins, holes and grooves before assembly (a hot blade or a quick pass with a heat gun).
- If a fit is too tight, first lower the flow by 2 %, then raise the clearance in processed/fits-PETG.json and finalize again.

Slicing and printing are your steps. Do not leave the printer unattended.
